# Financial and statistical assumptions

## Market features and forecasting

Features are close-of-observation calculations with trailing windows only. They support a decision made after that close; they do not imply intraday availability. Weekly/monthly defaults are 5/21 **observed sessions**, not calendar weeks/months. Suspensions and unidentified holidays are not forward-filled. The Saturday–Wednesday calendar applies only supplied exclusions and is explicitly estimated, including future forecast dates.

Annualization defaults to 252 and is configurable. This is a convention, not a verified count of Iranian sessions in any year. RSI, MACD, moving averages, Bollinger bands, ATR, volume/OBV, volatility, skew, kurtosis, autocorrelation, momentum and drawdown use historical inputs. NAV premium requires a same-date NAV; provider NAV timestamps still need review if using the feature before its actual publication.

Complete adjusted prices, when present, are used consistently for model features/targets. Partial adjusted histories are rejected. Without adjustments, the model explicitly records an unadjusted basis; corporate actions can distort returns. Forecast return paths are anchored to the latest **actual** close for option payoffs. This does not forecast future corporate actions, cash distributions, trading limits, suspensions or leverage resets. Known upcoming actions can invalidate that mapping and should be handled before using the scenarios.

The direct LSTM outputs future session log returns. Positive prices are formed with exponentiation; overflow/nonfinite paths fail rather than being clipped into a plausible output. CPU tests use fixed seeds; exact reproducibility across hardware/library versions is not promised. The model has configurable layers, hidden size, dropout, attention, lookback, learning rate and early stopping through `ModelConfig`.

Training/validation/calibration/test boundaries are chronological and target-purged. Overlapping origins within a partition remain dependent. Residual-vector resampling preserves empirical cross-horizon dependence but cannot invent unseen tail regimes. MC dropout is an approximate epistemic signal; residuals mix market innovations, parameter error and misspecification. The two uncertainty components are not an identifiable decomposition, and their combined intervals must be checked against reported held-out coverage. No normality assumption drives the predictor. Normality and candidate-distribution BIC diagnostics are descriptive because residual vectors overlap.

Persistence point-error scores use exactly the cutoff price. Its uncertainty bands use centered historical-return bootstraps; the historical-drift baseline uses uncentered training returns. Quantile coverage, quantile loss and empirical CRPS use their empirical sample distributions. The selected LSTM is not automatically preferred over either baseline.

## Strategies, capital and pricing

Every option leg has its own contract multiplier, signed side, count, premium and entry/exit fee. At a common expiration, option cash-equivalent P&L is signed intrinsic value minus signed premium and fees. Stock legs include their actual entry capital. Maxima/minima and break-evens are derived from the piecewise-linear payoff over all nonnegative underlying prices, including its unbounded right tail; a chart range never defines a theoretical maximum loss.

`stock_fees` are stock entry fees and `stock_exit_fees` are exit fees. Initial cash flow and paid capital include entry fees only; terminal/scenario P&L includes both sides.

Mixed underlying IDs/expirations are rejected. Templates have strike-order, width and multiplier constraints; custom legs can carry unequal multipliers. Expiration payoff for a physically settled contract describes economic intrinsic value and assumes exercise/funding/delivery are possible. It does not model delivery operations, borrowing, taxes, failed settlement, exercise notice cutoffs or assignment. The historical engine supports only verified European cash settlement. Short American positions are excluded from forecast-based decisions because early assignment is not modeled.

For European contracts before expiration, BSM assumes a lognormal pricing process, continuous compounding, ACT/365, constant volatility and continuous dividend yield. It prices options under pricing assumptions; **it does not provide the physical investment probability distribution**. Expected P&L is integrated over LSTM physical scenarios. The dashboard exposes annual rate/volatility and liquidation-cost scenarios; the Python API also exposes dividend yield. Constant assumed future IV and a proportional liquidation haircut are scenario assumptions, not guaranteed future bid/ask spreads. Unsupported American pre-expiration valuation fails explicitly. No inferred pricing engine is used for unknown contract styles.

Option IV uses same-date spot and reliable two-sided premiums only, strict no-arbitrage bounds and a finite numerical bracket. An unavailable IV remains blank. Last traded premiums are displayed as observations but never silently substituted for executable bid/ask. Bid/ask quantity units must be verified as contract counts. Entry slippage worsens buy and sell prices. Stock entries in covered strategies use the explicitly supplied entry price and stock fee; a user must account for stock execution liquidity separately.

For paid long options, capital is gross premium plus entry fees (and stock capital where applicable). Covered positions use committed stock capital plus long-option outlays/fees, without subtracting received call credit. Other short strategies require supplied verified margin/collateral. A zero, unknown or inappropriate denominator yields monetary P&L only. No financing cost, interest earned on collateral, variation-margin call dynamics or portfolio margin netting is included. Known broker capital can be less than eventual theoretical loss; the backtest flags realized breaches instead of inventing liquidation.

## Risk and rankings

Scenario expected P&L is the arithmetic mean, or the supplied probability-weighted sum. Expected percentage return is `100 * E[P&L] / capital`. Profit/loss probabilities use strict positive/negative P&L; zero-profit scenarios belong to neither. Target return and loss threshold probabilities are also strict inequalities. Empirical quantiles use the weighted inverse CDF.

Loss is `-P&L`. VaR is its chosen confidence quantile; CVaR integrates exactly the worst tail probability mass, including fractional mass at the boundary. Reported losses are floored at zero when every tail outcome is profitable. Risk metrics in the UI default to 95% confidence. Percentage quantiles and standard deviations are unavailable without positive capital. Monetary risk metrics remain usable.

Budget filtering excludes unknown capital. Ranking compares one horizon at a time and excludes explicitly hypothetical or infeasible strategies. The custom score min-max normalizes expected percentage return, profit probability and CVaR across eligible candidates; weights must be nonnegative and sum to one. Pareto dominance compares expected return against monetary CVaR. This relative score is not an absolute forecast quality or suitability rating.

## Backtesting and explicit limitations

The price walk-forward engine retrains on each historical prefix, never a random train/test split. When only a currently revised history is supplied, that is a retrospective price study and may still have corporate-action/survivorship bias. For strict option backtests, records must have been available at the cutoff; stored price vintages prevent late revisions from entering earlier forecasts. A historical option quote must exist for the exact contract and date. No current option chain is copied into the past.

The engine supports nonoverlapping trades, budget constraints, explicit entry/exit costs and slippage, known margins and same-date executable quotes/depth. Premature option exits require archived bid/ask; they do not use a guessed BSM mark as realized P&L. At expiry, an explicit exchange settlement observation is required. Missing observations and unsupported terms skip the trade and produce a reason. The default dashboard selector compares simple long calls and puts; the Python selection callback supports any eligible strategy.

Historical entries require a verified `execution_at` timestamp after every underlying input became available and no earlier than the option-chain information used for selection. A daily after-close signal cannot fill at an earlier closing quote. Multi-leg entries must be synchronized; missing or inconsistent timing disables the trade. There is no implicit next-session execution fallback. Stock-covered historical strategies are disabled because this implementation does not establish historical hedge-stock executions; their payoff and forward scenario analytics remain available. A missing-session mismatch between forecast target date and realized exit date also disables that comparison.

Equity is marked on realized exit dates, so the reported maximum drawdown is **not an intratrade marked-to-market drawdown**. Positions are not dynamically liquidated on margin changes. The engine does not simulate exchange order queues, price-limit fill probability, early exercise, stochastic IV, tick-size rounding, portfolio-level exposure netting, rolling positions, cross-expiry positions or real broker execution.

Only synthetic computational tests were available during implementation. Real-world forecast calibration, market-data continuity, actual contract specifications, full holiday coverage and realized strategy profitability require the user's actual datasets and credentials. Deployment authentication, reverse proxy, monitoring/alert delivery and disaster-recovery operations are outside this local application.
